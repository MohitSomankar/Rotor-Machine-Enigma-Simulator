ABOUT ENIGMA

The Enigma machine was an electro-mechanical rotor cipher device used by the German military in World War II to encrypt and decrypt secret messages. Each keypress routed an electrical signal through a plugboard, three rotating rotors, and a reflector — producing a different substitution cipher letter every single time, because the rotors physically stepped forward after every key.

This simulator reproduces the real Enigma M3 wiring: rotors I–V with their historical wirings and turnover notches, Reflector B, a 10-pair plugboard, and the famous double-stepping anomaly — a mechanical quirk where the middle rotor sometimes steps twice in a row.

Because Enigma is self-reciprocal, the exact same settings that encrypt a message will also decrypt it — which is why both operators (or laptops, in this project) must agree on the same Rotor Selection, Ring Settings, Initial Positions and Plugboard Pairs — the "Daily Key" — before exchanging messages.
